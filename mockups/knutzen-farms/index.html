Our Potatoes

Three colors, one valley

The Skagit Valley's soil and mild marine climate are ideal for specialty potatoes. We grow three, and we're obsessive about the quality of every pack.

Reds

Smooth, bright red skin with white flesh. The Skagit Valley's signature potato.

Whites

Thin, pale skin and creamy flesh. An all-purpose potato for the kitchen.

Yellows

Golden skin and buttery yellow flesh. Holds its shape roasted, boiled or mashed.

Where to find them

Our potatoes are sold at grocery stores and produce markets across the United States and served in restaurants around the country.

What's in the Dirt

A season on the farm

From cut seed to the packing shed, we handle every step ourselves. Here's how a crop comes together, measured in weeks after planting.

  1. Early spring

    Working the ground

    The soil is turned, prepared and refined before a single seed goes in.

  2. Planting

    Cut seed

    Seed potatoes are cut into pieces and loaded into the planter.

  3. 6 weeks

    First leaves

    Plants break through. Rototilling and weed control keep the rows clean.

  4. 8 weeks

    Timely water

    Irrigation and integrated pest management through the summer.

  5. 11 weeks

    Full bloom

    The fields turn pink and lavender. It's the view in our header.

  6. +4 weeks
    Vines down.
    Skins setting below.

    Skin set

    Vines are desiccated so the potatoes can set a firm skin before digging.

  7. Harvest

    Digging

    Harvesters lift the crop straight into trucks bound for storage.

  8. Packed

    Packed & shipped

    Washed, graded and packed on the farm, then loaded for the road.

Our Family

The knowledge of the past, with the insight of the future

Jess H. Knutzen came from Broager, Denmark and founded the farm in 1894.

He and his wife Metta raised seven sons and homesteaded the Olympic Marsh, then a stump-covered bay bed. Years of clearing turned it into a working farm they named Highland View.

Today the 5th and 6th generations farm the land their great-great-grandparents cleared. We're proud of the potatoes we grow here, and we'd love to share them with you.
